# An Unsuccessful Execution

*On a recent visit to Ross, and a trip out to Ledbury on the Daffodil community bus line, traditional storyteller Tony "Monty" Hirst came across this rather gruesome tale in the Butcher Row House Museum.*

As recent events in America show, execution attempts don't always result in the death of the condemned prisoner. On being hanged for the traditional time in 1736, Thomas Reynolds, of Ledbury, surprised his executioner by pushing open the lid of the coffin he had just been placed in.

Reynolds had been convicted for taking part in an attack on a turnpike in Ledbury on September 20th, 1735, a recently erected toll gate that required road users to pay for road usage. Upset at the introduction of this new tax, Reynolds was arrested in the first of two attacks on the turnpikes by large numbers of armed men, disguised in women's clothing and with faces blackened by charcoal.

As the *Gloucester Journal* of Friday, 3 August 1736, reported, the executioner, "thinking that foul Play, insisted upon hanging him up again, when the Mob taking a different Turn, cry’d *Save his Life*, and fell upon the poor Executioner (who stickled hard for fulfilling the Law) and beat him in a most miserable Manner; they carried the Prisoner to a House at Acton, where he was put to bed; he vomited three Pints of Blood, and 'twas believ’d would recover, but on giving him a Glass of Wine, he died."
