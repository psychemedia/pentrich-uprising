Leeds Mercury, published Saturday 13 June 1812, page 3


STEALING ARMS. We are greatly concerned to state, that this highly dangerous and most alarming species of de * predation continues to be pursued with increased audacity and success. In our last. we stated, generally, that several persons at Netherton, near Horbury, had been robbed of their fire-arms; but one instance (the particulars of which have since come to our knowledge,) is of so daring and atrocious a nature, as to require being stated somewhat in detail. Mr. Abraham Lees, an inoffensive old man, who lives at a lone house in Netherton, about two miles from Wakefield, was visited by a party of those nocturnal depredators about twelve o'clock oil the night of Wednesday the 3d instant. Incapable of making any resistance, he delivered up his fire-arms; they then demanded his money, and threatened him with instant death if fie hesitated; upon this threat lie gave them a guinea note and some silver, which was all the money he had in the house; they then insisted upon having the keys of his drawers delivered to them, which he was under the necessity of complying with. They then ransacked his drawers but not finding the treasure they expected, they behaved in so violent a manner as to excite an apprehension that murder was intended, and Mr. Lees, on his knees, begged for the life of himself and his sister. The intimidation created by this outrage was so strong, that Mr. Lees has left his house and gone to reside in a neighbouring town for security. On Thursday these depredators visited a number of houses at Osset, where they got front eight to ten guns; they also demanded, and its some cases obtained ammunition. Some delay having occurred at one house, they fired several balls through the door. On Saturday night, about ten o'clock, a number of men, with muskets, were seen performing the so litary exercise, at the skirts of Soothill-Wood, in the parish of West-Ardsley; and about two o'clock on Sunday morning, 47 of them, all arm- ed, passed through the village of Gawthorp, near Osset. where they obtained some guns, and several SumIs of money, which they are now in the habit of demanding. About half past twelve o'clock the same night, a number of these people knocked at the door of Mr. Israel Rhodes, of Lee-Pair, and demanded his gun, which he was obliged to surrender into their hands. They then proceeded to another house in the neighbourhood, and took from the family a gun; and afterwards to the house of Mr. Leathley, of the same place, and extorted from him two muskets a pistol, and a guinea. Several parties of the same description of per. ?? save also been active it, collecting arms in the neighbourhood of Holmfirth, almost every night since our last; but they have nut demanded money as in the neighbourhood of Horbury and Osset. Much has been soll about the prompt protection afforded by the British Government to the Portuguese, but, surely, it is full as necessary that protection should be afforded to British subjects, whose property and tives are daily outraged and endangered with impunity. This is a subject which, above all others, demands the prompt attention of Government. On Saturday last, the inhabitants of Horbury were much alarmed in consequence of a musket, loaded with ball, having beer, discharged, about eleven o'clock in the forenoon, near Mr. Foster, of that place, whose Mill was destroyed some time ago. Mr. Foster conceiving that this was done intentionally, applied to a Magistrate, who issued a warrant to apprehend the person who fired the piece, Joshua Naylor, of Horbury, tailor; but it appeared, on examination, before the. Rev. Mr. Taylor, that though Naylor had fired a double-barrelled gun loaded with ball, yet iis situation was such, that he could not have seen Mr. Foster, he was discharged, with a reprimand for his want if caution. One of the balls passed very near Mr. Foster's person. From Huddersfield, under the date of the I ith lust. our correspondent writes:- * The Arms-stealing system still prevails- several stand have been stolen this ?? Luddites are likewise very active in stealing lead- pipes, and some dying-vessels, made of lead, have been cut in pieces and carried away. In consequence of the Voluntary Association for watching and patroling, this town is kept very quiet ; but out of the limits of the patrole, depredations are committed every night."

In *Manchester Mercury*, [Tuesday 15 December 1812](https://www.britishnewspaperarchive.co.uk/viewer/bl/0000239/18121215/004/0003).

*Huddersfield, Dec. 3.*—Our Correspondent under the above date says, that the spirit of Luddism, which  his last letter, like the Prince Regent's speech represented to be extinct, had again appeared and raged with more than usual violence. Last Sunday night, about a quarter past nine o'clock, a number of men armed with pistols or short guns, one them the lower part his face covered with a black handkerchief, entered the house Mr. Wm. Walker, of New-Hall, Fartown, near Huddersfield, cloth manufacturer, and after taking from him a gun, a pistol and a powder horn, demanded his money, and obtained from him about fifteen pounds in notes, the whole of which they offered to return him, except one, if he would give them a guinea in gold; not being aware of this decoy, he took out a small purse containing five guineas, which they immediately seized; and took all the gold, without returning the notes: the chief then proceeded to ransack his papers, while others of the party presented their pieces at Mr. Walker, and after cautioning the family, on pain of death, not to quit the house for two hours after, they departed.

The same gang then proceeded to the house cf Mr. George Scholes, a shop-keeper, at Fartown, from which they took gun, between 30 and 40 shillings in silver, one five-pound note, and 9 or 10 provincial one pound notes, together with a pair of silver tea spoons; not content with this booty they went into the cellar, and seized and carried away a bottle of rum, and some provisions. On taking their leave they told the family, that they should leave a guard about the house to prevent any one quitting it tor two hours. After their departure, it was found that they had left behind them a sort of blunderbuss, and a hatchet.

From thence they proceeded to the house Mr. Josias Thornton, of Gilly Royd, near Fixby, farmer, where they arrived about half past ten o'clock: here four men entered the house, two them armed with blunderbusses, a third with a gun, and the other with a pistol; their first demand was for arms, but on being told the family had neither arms nor money they immediately ordered "Enoch, Captain, Serjeant, and Hatchet-men to enter," and two others entered the house, but on Mrs Thornton promising to endeavour to find them some money, they returned at the word of command; she then took a pocket book containing five one pound notes from a drawer, which one of them snatched from her, while others were employed as guards over Mr. Thornton; all this party was disguised, and one of these wretches struck Mrs. Thornton a violent blow over the head, and another of them snapped a pistol, which did not explode, at her husband.

They next proceeded to the house of Mr. James Brook, of Bracken Hall, in Fartown, where, after conducting themselves in the same outrageous manner, they carried away his silver watch, a one pound note, and four shillings in silver. From thence they proceeded to Mr. John Wood's, where, after breaking the door and windows, and rumaging the house without finding any thing that answered their purpose, they called over the numbers from 1 to 9, and went away. From John Wood's they went to the house of Mr. Wm. Radcliffe, at Woodside, where they obtained £11 10 6 in notes, six guineas in gold, and between two and three pounds in silver and copper, besides a quantity of tea, some loaf sugar and several articles of plate. Their last visit was to Moses Ball, Gilly Royd, whom they plundered of about two pounds worth of silver change. The number of this desperate banditti is not precisely known, but supposed to be about nine or ten. Several persons have been apprehended on suspicion of having been concerned in the above robberies, but the result have not heard.— *Leeds Mercury.*
