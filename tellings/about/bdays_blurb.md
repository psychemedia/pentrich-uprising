__Protest Days, Rebellion Nights — Tales of the Luddite Times__

*They were days of direct action: the Diggers and the Levellers, the Plug Drawer rioters and the Pentrich rebels; the followers of King Ludd, and Captain Swing; of juries, and spies; of executions, and alibis. They were were the days we now remember through stories.*

The period 1811 to 1817 was just such a period in British history. The Luddite times, when the framework knitters, the croppers, and the hand-loom weavers, skilled workers all, stood up against the rash and hasty adoption of the machines that would threaten their very existence. Some of the tales are well known — the attack on Cartwright's mill, and the murder of William Horsfall. The show trial at York; then later, the march of the Blanketeers in Manchester and the "revolution" in Pentrich, the village that was home to many of the original Luddite attacks several years before. Other tales are perhaps less well known — the Loughborough mill attack, and the Folly Hall revolution.

So come, hear tell, some of the stories, some of the tales, of those troubled times, all told in a traditional way.

*Tony "Monty" Hirst, aka Monty storyteller, is a traditional storyteller based on the Isle of Wight. You might already have come across him at Beautiful Days telling pop-up folk tales in the Magic Teapot and at the Tiny Tea Tent.*
